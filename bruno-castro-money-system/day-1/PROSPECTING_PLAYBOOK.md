# PROSPECTING PLAYBOOK

How to get from 10 seeded rows to 200 qualified prospects, and what to send them.

---

## What is already in `prospects.csv`, and what is not

**Seeded: 10 real companies.** Five are **verified beMatrix partners**, named in
beMatrix's own partner and beMaster network material. Five are UK stand builders
listed as ESSA members.

**Deliberately left blank: emails and contact names.** I will not invent those.
A guessed email address bounces and damages your sending domain; a guessed
contact name gets your message deleted. Every blank field is a field you fill
from the company's own website or LinkedIn, which takes about two minutes per
company.

The `bematrix_partner` column is either `YES` with the evidence, or `VERIFY`. It
is never a guess.

## Where the other 190 come from

### Tier 1 — beMatrix partners (highest value, do this first)

The whole point of your specialisation is that this list is **finite and
published**. There are hundreds of certified partners worldwide, not thousands of
anonymous prospects.

- **beMasters network** — beMatrix's own listing of its best builders: <https://bematrix.com/eu/en/support/bemasters>
- **bePartner network** — the wider partner and rental network across Europe, the UK and the US: <https://bematrix.com/eu/en>
- beMatrix's site has a form to be connected with partners. Use it as a designer offering capacity, not as a buyer.
- **LinkedIn** — search `beMatrix` and filter to People, then to companies. Anyone whose profile mentions beMatrix works with the system.

Target: **40 companies.** These convert at several times the rate of a generic
stand builder, because you speak their system.

### Tier 2 — UK stand builders and contractors

- **ESSA members directory** — the Event Supplier and Services Association, the UK trade body. Every member is a vetted supplier: <https://www.essa.uk.com/members>
- ESSA's stand contractor listing specifically: <https://www.essa.uk.com/event-suppliers/70-use-an-essa-member/1341-welcome-to-the-stand-contractors-supplier-listing>
- ESSA's bespoke stand design and manufacture listing: <https://www.essa.uk.com/event-suppliers/1271-welcome-to-the-design-manufacture-bespoke-stands-supplier-listing>
- **AEO and AEV** member directories carry the same list from the organiser and venue side.

Target: **80 companies.**

### Tier 3 — Europe and the Gulf

- Exhibitor and supplier directories of the shows themselves. Every major show publishes its official contractor list — NEC Birmingham, ExCeL London, Messe Frankfurt, Messe Düsseldorf, RAI Amsterdam, Fira Barcelona, Dubai World Trade Centre.
- German and Dutch builders are worth the extra effort: high budgets, and many are looking for English-speaking design capacity.

Target: **50 companies.**

### Tier 4 — the undefended niche

Brazilian companies exhibiting in Europe. Native Portuguese, based in the UK,
ten years of European show floors — you have no meaningful competition here.

- **ApexBrasil** publishes which Brazilian companies are exhibiting at which international shows.
- Brazilian chambers of commerce in the UK and EU.
- Brazilian business groups on LinkedIn and WhatsApp.

Target: **30 companies.** Expect the highest reply rate of any tier, because
you will be the only person who contacts them in their own language.

## Finding the right person

Never send to `info@`. Two minutes per company:

1. **LinkedIn company page → People → filter by title.** In order of usefulness:
   *Design Manager · Head of Design · Studio Manager · Project Manager · Operations Director · Managing Director* (small firms: go straight to the MD).
2. **Email format.** Find one published address on the site, deduce the pattern
   (`first.last@`, `first@`, `finitial+last@`), apply it. If you cannot find a
   single published address, leave it blank and send via LinkedIn instead.
3. **Never guess a pattern from nothing.** Blank beats bounced.

Record the source in the `source` column. When you later ask "which segment
answers", that column is the answer.

## The hook: a free 3D concept in 90 minutes

This is the entire reason your reply rate can beat the industry's 2–4%. Almost
nobody sends work before being hired.

**The procedure:**

1. Find a show the company is actually working — their site, LinkedIn, or the
   show's exhibitor list.
2. Pick a plausible stand size. 3×3 or 6×3 is safe.
3. Build a concept in SketchUp. **Ninety minutes, hard stop.** It is a
   conversation opener, not a deliverable.
4. One render, exported clean. If they run beMatrix, do it in beMatrix frames and
   say so — that single detail is what makes it undeniable.
5. Attach it to the first email.

**Timebox it ruthlessly.** Twenty of these a day is impossible; that is not the
model. Send the concept to your **Tier 1 and priority A** targets only —
roughly 40 companies — and use plain text for the rest.

Never claim the concept is for a real brief you were given. It is speculative
work, described as speculative work.

> Hi [NAME], following up on the note below in case it arrived at a bad moment.
>
> One question rather than a pitch: who handles design overflow when you are at
> capacity during show season? If it is not you, a name would be genuinely

>

### Follow-up 2 — day 11, final

> Hi [NAME], last one from me.
>
> I will assume the timing is wrong rather than keep filling your inbox. If
> capacity gets tight later in the season, the portfolio is at [URL] and I am a
> reply away.
>
> All the best,
> Bruno

**Three touches, then stop.** A fourth converts almost nobody and costs you the
relationship. Move them to `follow_up` stage with a date three months out.

### Reactivation — previous clients and contacts

> Hi [NAME], it has been a while. I am taking on freelance exhibition and design
> work again, and thought of you.
>
> If you have anything coming up — stand design, drawings, artwork — I have
> capacity from [DATE]. And if you know anyone short on design hours this season,
> an introduction would mean a lot.
>
> Bruno

**Send this one first, on day one, to everyone you have ever worked with.** It is
the highest-converting message in this document and takes an afternoon.

## Staying legal

You are contacting UK and EU businesses, so this matters — and it is also your
own instruction: no illegal automation, no spam.

- **Corporate subscribers** — limited companies and LLPs — can be sent B2B
  marketing email under UK PECR without prior consent, relying on legitimate
  interest under GDPR. **Sole traders and partnerships count as individual
  subscribers** and have stronger protection; be more careful there.
- **Always** identify yourself, give a real reply address, and include a way to
  opt out. Every template above has one. Honour it immediately.
- Keep a note of **why** each contact is relevant to your service. That is what
  legitimate interest means in practice, and the `service_pitch` column records it.
- **Do not scrape** anything in breach of a site's terms, and do not buy lists.
  Manual research from published directories is both legal and higher quality.
- Send from a real domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured. Twenty a day from
  a properly configured domain lands; two hundred from a new one goes to spam.

This is general guidance rather than legal advice. If you scale past a few
hundred contacts a month, spend an hour with the ICO's direct marketing guidance.

## Daily routine

| Time | Task | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 30 min | Research and add new prospects | 20 rows |
| 45 min | First-contact messages | 10 sent |
| 20 min | Follow-ups due today | 5 sent |
| 90 min | Free 3D concepts for priority A targets | 1 per day |
| 10 min | Update the CSV: stage, last contact, next follow-up | — |

Roughly three hours a day. Everything else — the portfolio, the CRM, the
platform — is secondary to this block.

## Measure only these

Weekly, in one line:

```
Contacts sent · Replies · Reply rate · Qualified · Proposals · Wins · £ won
```

If reply rate is under 4% after 100 contacts, the message is wrong, not the list.
Change the subject line first, then the opening sentence, then the offer — one
variable at a time.
